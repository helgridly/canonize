## Introduction

I did some research on meditation spots in the city for a friend. Two caveats:
1. These are all Buddhist places. Other meditation traditions exist, I just have no experience with them
2. My background is in Zen so I have a significant Zen frame and bias

This is an oversimplification but you are broadly going to see 3 traditions: Vipassana / insight, Zen, and Tibetan.

## Vipassana

Has IMO two flavours: a very deliberate, precise system with flowcharts and extremely specific instructions saying do X, once you notice Y, change your practice to Z; or late gen X / aging boomer hippie floaty vibes with lots of conversation about people's feelings and troubles and a very loose approach to practice.

#### New York Vipassana / Dhamma House (Goenka tradition), Midtown
[[Map](https://maps.app.goo.gl/7hDHE8z6pzx9oniH7), [website](https://www.ny.us.dhamma.org/)]

Deliberate, precise flavour. They are known for running free retreats all around the world with a very specific body scan sequence that they are very strict about. During the retreats they wheel out a TV and put on a video recorded 40 years ago of a guy who is now dead telling you what to do; then you go do it for a couple of days, then everyone gathers again for a new video with different instructions. The retreats are often people's first introduction to meditation and a common response is "that was intense and pretty fucking horrible but I got some stuff out of it and I now want to explore other things". How that translates to a drop in 2 hour evening program I have no idea, it might be less stressful.

#### New York Insight, Midtown
[[Map](https://maps.app.goo.gl/KdiqvBpbju16My6g7), [website](https://www.nyimc.org/)]

Boomer hippie flavour. It will at least be a pleasant experience and a chill time. Potentially an easy entryway and maybe a good spot if you like the vibes.

## Zen

IMO Korean Zen is the most accessible, they tend to be sweet (and have an endearing idiosyncratic rhetorical style in their talks). There are two kinds of Japanese Zen, Soto and Rinzai. Rinzai wants very much for you to wake up and get enlightened and is very Type A about it. Soto thinks if you're too loud you'll scare away the enlightenment so they want you to sit very intently while insisting you're doing nothing at all for no reason. The thing I love about Zen is that frequent 1-1s with a teacher are a part of the practice, so you have someone to talk to very directly who can help guide you. Over time you get to know each other and it can be really beautiful.

#### Jo-Gei, deep UWS (96th st)
[[Map](https://maps.app.goo.gl/RwxKVHkoWCRnwUrD8), [website](https://nyjogeitemple.com/)]

Korean Zen. I like these guys, their Tuesday night group skews pretty young & really diverse, 1 white person in a group of 14! (American Buddhism is extremely white so this was a welcome change for me.) Felt like a sweet group, I liked them a lot & would recommend.

## Tibetan

Esoteric, can be kinda chaotic and overwhelming: gods and monsters and inscrutable rituals, strong emphasis on their spiritual leader as a holy, almost magical person. Ultimately the base practice is pretty normal but the setting for it is kinda insane, which might either be fun or way too weird / distracting. I know shit all about Tibetan practice beyond a sense of legit vs. culty so these are just links of ones that seem legit:

## Proceed with caution

* Kadampa -- there's one in wburg on the corner of mccarren
* Soka Gakkai / SGI-USA -- ~Union Sq
* Shinnyo Center -- Midtown
* Won Buddhism -- UES
* Shambhala -- Prospect Heights

The first 4 are "new religious movements" which are sorta Buddhism-inspired but have weirdnesses around their practice that the traditional forms they spun off from are very mad about. Shambhala is a legitimate flavour of Tibetan Buddhism but the guy who started it slept with a lot of his students, then passed the torch onto his son. This sort of fuckery is endemic within Buddhist groups (particularly in Tibetan lineages, often Zen too, due to close student/teacher relationships), but Shambhala in particular still really likes to venerate their leaders and IMO hasn't reckoned with it in a mature way. Can feel a bit culty.

It's possible that all of these are welcoming places where you will have a good experience. It's also possible that that's true _and_ they are a slow escalator into a cult. So I am not saying avoid, just proceed with caution, which you should generally do anyway. Not that this is a space where you must absolutely keep your wits about you at all times, just trust your intuition of what feels good for you, whether what they're saying makes sense / resonates with you / is something you're interested in trying. If not, just peace out and find somewhere else.

"Meditation groups" are ripe for weird culty shit in general; I found many while putting together this list. So if you DYOR and find somewhere, I would advise checking their website. If it's a rich white lady either talking about manifesting, or bragging that she's worked with tech companies, it's some commercialized new age hippie bullshit. The group should say who their teachers are and contextualize their practice in some kind of lineage or tradition. If they're not doing that but their website looks really slick and modern it's a bit suspicious; a website that's usable but looks a bit shit is a good sign that they care about their practice more than their marketing.
